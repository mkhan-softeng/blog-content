Some of my software engineering colleagues recently shared how amazed, and at the same time concerned, they were by how capable AI coding agents have become.

These agents are now completing what would traditionally take days, and in some cases weeks, of work in just a few hours.

Naturally, the question many people are asking is: are our jobs still secure?

As a senior software engineer myself, take this with a grain of salt. But here is what I am genuinely observing.

## Progress has always worked this way

To some extent, this can be compared to how cloud computing transformed infrastructure engineering, how heavy machinery reshaped farming and logging, or how calculators enhanced the work of accountants.

Humanity continuously builds tools on top of previous tools to make work faster, easier, and more efficient.

## What AI agents do well

AI coding agents are already exceptional in many respects. They can:

- Generate code and build applications.
- Write tests and refactor systems.
- Automate repetitive tasks.
- Accelerate development at remarkable speed.

However, they still need to be orchestrated.

## Where AI agents still fall short

While these agents are highly capable, they remain limited in several critical areas, including:

- Understanding nuanced business requirements.
- Making architectural decisions that hold up over many years.
- Maintaining complex systems through changing teams and priorities.
- Security reasoning in real production environments.
- Stakeholder communication and production accountability.
- Debugging distributed systems under pressure.
- Prioritisation and product judgement.
- Knowing when not to build something.

The key point is that AI agents still require direction, oversight, and experience.

## Strong engineers orchestrate, they do not "vibe code"

Strong software engineers do not simply "vibe code" and hope for the best. They guide and orchestrate these agents using years of engineering judgement, business understanding, operational awareness, and architectural thinking.

A simple way to think about it is this.

One AI agent may be capable of building a small shed. However, building a high rise tower requires planning, architecture, coordination, governance, security, operational thinking, and experienced software engineers guiding the process.

That is where experienced senior software engineers continue to shine.

## The role is being transformed, not removed

In many ways, this shift is not removing engineering roles. It is transforming them.

Higher level engineering work becomes even more valuable, including:

- System design.
- Infrastructure and security.
- Workflow integration.
- Performance and cost optimisation.
- Governance.
- Effectively coordinating between humans and AI agents.

## Where the impact will be felt first

The group most likely to feel the impact first includes junior developers entering the industry, as well as software engineers working in areas that involve highly repetitive or narrowly specialised implementation work. This includes routine frontend development, backend CRUD applications, manual testing, and similar tasks that AI agents are rapidly becoming capable of handling.

That is where much of the concern comes from.

Traditionally, junior developers learned through smaller implementation tasks and repetitive coding work. These are precisely the areas where AI is now becoming extremely effective.

The shift that needs to happen is for software engineers to not only learn foundational engineering skills, but also how to effectively work alongside AI agents under the guidance of experienced engineers.

## Education is already adapting

Interestingly, I recently spoke with one of the lecturers at the University of Queensland.

UQ, along with many other universities I presume, is already recognising this shift and beginning to adapt aspects of its software engineering programs. Students are now being taught not only core engineering fundamentals, but also how to effectively use AI models within modern software development workflows. Increasingly, they are also learning how to work with the agents built on top of those models.

In other words, how to orchestrate them effectively.

## The road ahead

The future may not belong to software engineers who compete against AI.

It will likely belong to software engineers who learn how to orchestrate it effectively.
